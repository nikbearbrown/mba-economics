# Pantry — Chapter 12: Environmental Protection and Negative Externalities

## Cold-open scenes
- 1952 Great London Smog. ~4,000–12,000 deaths. UK Clean Air Act 1956 followed.

## Worked examples
- SO₂ cap-and-trade (1990 Clean Air Act amendments) — 65% reduction at lower cost than C&C.
- EU ETS for carbon.
- Coase theorem with two adjacent properties.

## Concept specifications
- **Externality:** a cost (or benefit) imposed on third parties not captured in the market price.
- **Private cost vs social cost.** Negative externality: private < social; market overproduces.
- **Pigouvian tax = external damage per unit.** Internalizes the externality.
- **Coase theorem:** efficient outcome regardless of who holds rights, *if* transaction costs are zero.
- **Climate is global; policy is national** — international free-rider problem.

## Trade-offs named
- Cost-effectiveness (market instruments) vs. quantity certainty (cap-and-trade) vs. price certainty (tax).
- Command-and-control predictability vs. inefficiency.
- Coase theorem elegance vs. transaction-cost reality.
- Domestic action vs. international free-riding.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Markets should handle externalities" → No, externalities are exactly where markets *don't* internalize the relevant costs.
- "Regulation is always better than markets for pollution" → No, market instruments often beat C&C on cost.
- "Coase shows we don't need government" → No, Coase requires clear property rights and low transaction costs, which government usually has to establish.
