# Pantry — Chapter 34: Globalization and Protectionism

## Cold-open scenes
- 2016 Brexit vote.
- 2018–19 U.S. tariffs on $360B Chinese imports + retaliation.

## Worked examples
- Tariff supply-and-demand calculation (price, quantity, revenue, deadweight loss).
- 25% tariff on hypothetical good: price rises, imports fall, deadweight loss.

## Concept specifications
- **Tariffs and quotas** raise domestic prices, transfer surplus, create deadweight loss.
- **Concentrated losses, dispersed gains** is the political-economy of trade (mirror of Ch 18).
- **Trade adjustment assistance** is the textbook policy response; empirically inadequate in the U.S.
- **Standard arguments for protection** (national security, infant industry, dumping, environmental/labor) each have theoretical grounds but practical limits.

## Trade-offs named
- Aggregate gains from trade vs. concentrated losses.
- Free trade efficiency vs. national security.
- Infant-industry protection vs. permanent rent-seeking.
- Trade liberalization speed vs. adjustment capacity.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Free trade hurts workers." → It hurts *some* workers; the aggregate gain is real; the distributional response has been weak.
- "Tariffs save American jobs." → Save some; cost more (consumers, downstream industries, retaliation).
- "WTO has been good for everyone." → Aggregate yes; specific industries and communities, no.
