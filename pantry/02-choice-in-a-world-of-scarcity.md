# Pantry — Chapter 2: Choice in a World of Scarcity

## Cold-open scenes
- **The 2020 BLS earnings gap** — $1,545/wk master's, $1,305/wk bachelor's, $781/wk high school. Yet only 36% have a bachelor's. Puzzle that frames the chapter.
- **Alphonso's $10 budget** — burgers and bus tickets. Anchor for budget constraint and opportunity cost.
- **Selena's bad movie** — sunk cost canonical example.

## Worked examples
- Alphonso budget math: $10/$2 = 5 burgers max; $10/$0.50 = 20 bus tickets max. Slope: −4 bus tickets/burger.
- $5/day lunch decision compounded: 250 days × $5 = $1,250/yr.
- Healthcare-vs-education PPF — used to introduce productive vs allocative efficiency.
- Brazil sugarcane vs U.S. wheat — comparative advantage from PPF curvature.

## Concept specifications
- **Opportunity cost ≠ accounting cost.** The factory's owned building has zero rent and a real opportunity cost.
- **Marginal ≠ total.** Compare the *next bite*, not the *whole meal*.
- **Sunk costs are gone.** They cannot affect any decision you make now.
- **Productive efficiency ≠ allocative efficiency.** On the curve = no waste. Right point on the curve = matches what society wants.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "People don't actually do this calculation, so the model is wrong" → No, the basketball player doesn't calculate trajectory either. Patterns of behavior, not internal calculation.
- "Sunk costs should matter — you've already invested" → No. The past doesn't get a vote.
- "More is better" → No. Diminishing marginal utility.
