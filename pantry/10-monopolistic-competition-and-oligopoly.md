# Pantry — Chapter 10: Monopolistic Competition and Oligopoly

## Cold-open scenes
- 1997–2004 French laundry-detergent cartel — P&G, Henkel, Unilever, Colgate-Palmolive. €361M fine 2011.

## Worked examples
- 600,000+ U.S. restaurants as monopolistic-competition exemplar.
- Prisoner's dilemma classic crime story + oligopoly payoff matrix.

## Concept specifications
- **Monopolistic competition:** many firms + differentiated products + free entry.
- **Long-run equilibrium has zero economic profit** but production is at less than min ATC ⇒ excess capacity.
- **Oligopoly:** few firms, strategic interaction.
- **Cartels are unstable** because individual incentive is to defect.
- **Game theory needed** to model oligopoly — multiple models (Cournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg) give different predictions.

## Named tools
- Differentiated demand (downward but flatter than monopoly).
- Tangency condition for monopolistic competition long-run equilibrium.
- Prisoner's dilemma payoff matrix.
- Dominant strategy.
- Cartel.
- Cournot, Bertrand, Stackelberg models (named, not formalized).

## Trade-offs named
- Variety vs. efficiency (mon comp).
- Cooperation vs. defection (oligopoly).
- Advertising as information vs. wasteful arms race.
- Stability of cartel vs. legality and detection.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Monopolistic competition is just inefficient." → Trade off variety for efficiency; values question.
- "Cartels last because firms cooperate." → No, they're inherently unstable; require monitoring or regulation to fail.
- "Advertising is pure waste." → Sometimes; sometimes informational; depends on the category.
