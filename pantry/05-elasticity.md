# Pantry — Chapter 5: Elasticity

## Cold-open scenes
- **2011 Netflix 60% price hike** — chapter opener; revisited in synthesis to illustrate inelastic-now, elastic-later.

## Worked examples
- Netflix as inelastic-2011, elastic-2024 due to substitutes appearing.
- 1973 oil embargo: tiny short-run elasticity, large 20-year elasticity.
- Cigarette tax incidence — addicted buyers absorb most of the tax.
- Beachfront vs. pizza supply — extreme cases of inelastic vs. elastic supply.

## Concept specifications
- **Elasticity is a ratio of percent changes**, not a slope.
- **Elasticity ≠ slope.** A demand curve with constant slope has different elasticities at different points.
- **Elasticity is conventionally absolute-valued** for demand (the negative sign is suppressed).
- **Time horizon matters.** Short-run vs. long-run elasticities are often very different.
- **Tax incidence falls on the more inelastic side.** "The side that can't leave pays."

## Named tools
- Price elasticity of demand (and supply).
- Income elasticity.
- Cross-price elasticity.
- Total-revenue test.
- Tax-incidence rule.
- Perfect elasticity (horizontal); perfect inelasticity (vertical).

## Trade-offs named
- Revenue maximization vs. behavior modification — same tax can be a great revenue tool *and* a poor behavior tool when demand is inelastic.
- Equity vs. revenue — taxes on inelastic goods raise revenue easily but fall on captive consumers.
- Short-run prediction reliability vs. long-run adjustment.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Raising prices loses customers, period." → Only when demand is elastic.
- "A tax legally on the seller is paid by the seller." → No, incidence depends on relative elasticities.
- "Elasticity is a property of a good." → No. Property of a market situation, including time horizon and available substitutes.
