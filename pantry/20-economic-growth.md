# Pantry — Chapter 20: Economic Growth

## Cold-open scenes
- DeLong calorie statistic: 2,500 (1600s) → 3,500 (1800) → 35,000 (2000) — the modern-growth story in one number.

## Worked examples

- Growth accounting decomposition: ~1/3 capital, ~1/5 human capital, ~1/2 TFP for U.S.
- South Korea vs. Philippines divergence over 60 years.

## Concept specifications
- **Modern growth ≈ post-1800.** Pre-modern: subsistence with sporadic improvement. Modern: sustained 1–2%+ for centuries.
- **Y = A · F(K, L, H).** Capital, labor, human capital, plus TFP (the residual we can't measure directly).
- **Diminishing returns to capital** = Solow framework's central insight.
- **Capital deepening** drives catch-up; **technology** drives frontier growth.
- **Convergence is conditional.** Requires institutional foundations.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Most countries are getting richer naturally." → Some are; some aren't. Convergence is conditional.
- "Capital is what makes us rich." → Partial. Diminishing returns; institutions and tech matter more in the long run.
- "Technology will save us." → Maybe. The post-2005 productivity slowdown is an open puzzle.
