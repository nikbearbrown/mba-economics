## Concept specifications
- **Say's Law** (long run, supply-side): supply creates its own demand.
- **Keynes's Law** (short run, demand-side): demand creates its own supply.
- **AD slopes down** because of wealth effect, interest rate effect, net export effect.
- **SRAS three zones**: Keynesian (flat, slack), intermediate (sloping), neoclassical (vertical, full employment).
- **LRAS vertical** at potential GDP — long-run output is supply-determined.

## Named tools
- Aggregate demand curve.
- Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) curve.
- Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS) curve.

- Stagflation.

## Trade-offs named
- Short-run stabilization vs. long-run supply enhancement.
- Demand stimulus effective in slack but inflationary at full employment.
- Speed of long-run adjustment vs. cost of short-run unemployment.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Stimulus always works." → Only in Keynesian zone; inflationary at full employment.
- "Markets always clear quickly." → They sometimes do, sometimes don't; depends on context.
- "Supply-side always matters most." → Long-run yes; short-run no.
