## Concept specifications
- **Rational ignorance** = voters' cost of being informed exceeds their personal benefit; structural, not moral.
- **Concentrated benefits, dispersed costs** = standard pattern that produces inefficient transfers.
- **Median voter theorem** holds under restrictive assumptions; useful baseline.
- **Voting paradox** (Condorcet): pairwise majorities can be cyclical; agenda-setter has power.
- **Arrow's impossibility theorem**: no voting system perfectly aggregates preferences.

## Trade-offs named
- Market failure (Ch 9–16) vs. government failure (this chapter).
- Concentrated influence vs. dispersed welfare.
- Centralized procedural rules (efficiency) vs. localized democratic responsiveness.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Voters are stupid." → No, they're rationally responding to costs and benefits of information.
- "Markets always fail; government solves." → No, government has its own structural failures.
- "Government always fails; markets solve." → Same answer in mirror.
