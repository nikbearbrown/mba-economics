# Pantry — Chapter 32: Macroeconomic Policy Around the World

## Cold-open scenes
- South Korea $158 (1960) → $35,000 (2024) GDP per capita.
- Comparison with Ghana, Burma, Philippines that started similarly and didn't catch up.

## Worked examples
- East Asian Tigers (Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, HK) sustained-growth model.
- China's reform path 1980 onward.
- Eastern Europe transition from central planning post-1990.

## Concept specifications
- **Income classification** by GNI per capita: low / lower-middle / upper-middle / high.
- **Middle-income trap** — stalling around $5K-$15K per capita.
- **Resource curse** — paradox of resource-rich countries staying poor.
- **Underemployment** — official unemployment may be low but productive employment also low.
- **Reform sequencing** matters as much as the reforms themselves.

## Named tools
- World Bank income classifications.
- East Asian Tigers model.
- Middle-income trap concept.
- Resource curse.
- Market-oriented reform package.
- Institutional capacity as macro determinant.

## Trade-offs named
- Catch-up growth (capital accumulation) vs. frontier growth (innovation).
- Open economy benefits vs. external shock vulnerability.
- Government intervention (state-led development) vs. market-led growth.
- Reform speed vs. social stability.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "All countries should follow the same policy menu." → No, context matters.
- "Markets always lead to growth." → Only with appropriate institutions.
- "East Asian model is universally replicable." → Contested; specific historical conditions mattered.
