## Concept specifications
- **Utility ≠ measurable; it's a model.** Economists use it as if it's measurable to derive predictions; the predictions are what matter.
- **Equimarginal rule:** MU/P equal across goods at the optimum.
- **Substitution effect:** relative-price effect; always toward the cheaper good.
- **Income effect:** real-income effect; usually reinforces substitution; can reverse for inferior goods.
- **Loss aversion ≠ risk aversion.** Loss aversion is about a reference point; risk aversion is about the curvature of utility.
- **Present bias ≠ patience.** Present bias is *time-inconsistent* preferences.

## Trade-offs named
- Standard model parsimony vs. behavioral realism — clean but wrong magnitudes vs. messy but better fit.
- Now-self vs. later-self — same person, different preferences depending on when "now" is.
- Default-driven choice vs. autonomy — defaults shape choices; whose values does the default reflect?

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "People are rational." → Approximately, on direction; less so on magnitude; systematically less so for present bias and loss aversion.
- "Behavioral economics shows the model is wrong." → Says the model is *incomplete*. Direction usually right; magnitude often biased.
- "Framing is just packaging." → No. Framing changes choices.
