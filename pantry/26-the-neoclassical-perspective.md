# Pantry — Chapter 26: Neoclassical Perspective

## Cold-open scenes
- Volcker 1979–82 disinflation: rates near 20%, unemployment to 10.8%, inflation crushed.
- 1970s stagflation as Keynesian failure.

## Worked examples
- Lucas critique applied to monetary policy.
- Trade-off between supply-side (durable) and demand-side (short-run) interventions.

## Concept specifications
- **Long run = supply-determined.** Both schools agree.
- **Short run** is where the disagreement lives.
- **Rational expectations** = agents use available information; predictable policy is anticipated.
- **Time-inconsistency** problem in policy; argues for rules over discretion.
- **Modern synthesis** combines sticky-price short run with rational long run.

## Named tools
- Long-run AS (LRAS).
- Rational expectations.
- Lucas critique.
- Time-inconsistency.
- Rules vs. discretion debate.
- New Keynesian / modern synthesis.

## Trade-offs named
- Supply-side reform durability vs. short-run cost of recession.
- Discretion (responsiveness) vs. rules (credibility).
- Inflation control benefits vs. recession costs of disinflation.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Wages and prices never adjust." → They do, eventually. The question is how fast.
- "Demand stimulus is always good." → No, not at full employment.
- "Supply side is everything." → Long-run yes, short-run no.
