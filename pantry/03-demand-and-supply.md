## Worked examples
- Apple price as joint demand-shift-right + supply-shift-left.

- Drought + ethanol mandate corn problem (LLM Exercise 5) — two shifts, ambiguous quantity.

## Concept specifications
- **Demand ≠ wanting.** Requires willingness *and* ability to pay.
- **Movement along ≠ shift of.** Price changes move along; everything else shifts.
- **Shortage ≠ scarcity.** Shortage is a market gap from price control or transient mismatch; scarcity is the underlying condition.
- **Equilibrium maximizes total surplus** — under the competitive-market assumptions only.

## Named tools
- Demand curve (downward-sloping).
- Supply curve (upward-sloping).
- Equilibrium price and quantity.
- Four-step analysis: baseline → identify change → shift the curve → read new equilibrium.
- Consumer surplus, producer surplus, total surplus.
- Deadweight loss.
- Price ceiling, price floor.

## Trade-offs named
- Price ceilings transfer surplus *and* destroy some — deadweight loss is the price.
- Price floors create surpluses (e.g., labor surplus = unemployment) under competitive assumption.
- Quick predictive direction vs. unknown magnitude — model is robust on direction, weak on size.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Higher organic prices = profiteering" → No. Joint demand-right + supply-left shifts.
- "Rent control helps renters" → Helps the renters who get apartments. Not the ones in the queue, the ones the building never gets built for, the ones the landlord screens out.
- "Minimum wage causes unemployment, period" → Direction right under competitive assumption; empirically messier in monopsonistic local markets.
