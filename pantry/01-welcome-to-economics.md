# Pantry — Chapter 1: Welcome to Economics

## Cold-open scenes
- **Adam Smith, 1776, in the pin workshop.** Notebook in hand, counting eighteen tasks. 4,800 vs. 20 — factor of 240. Reusable for: any chapter on productivity, division of labor, or industrial organization.
- **Pioneer family, 1840 Iowa.** Generalists; richer in skills, poorer in goods. Historical control case for specialization gains.
- **Restaurant kitchen at dinner service.** Six workers, eight strides across. The chapter's anchor analogy. Reusable wherever a small system needs to model a larger economy.
- **2021 chip shortage.** Used in synthesis to scale specialization-and-trade brittleness up to global supply chains. Hsinchu earthquake / fab-fire imagery.

## Worked examples (in prose)
- **Pin productivity.** 10 workers × 4,800 pins/day = 48,000 pins/day. Solo craftsman = 20/day. 240× ratio.
- **Surgeon and carpenter.** Surgeon better at both, but should hire carpenter — comparative advantage canonical.
- **Honda Civic vs. custom car.** Economies of scale made tangible.
- **Cook A vs. Cook B (in LLM Exercise 4).** A: 6 salads/hr or 4 entrées/hr. B: 2 salads/hr or 3 entrées/hr.

## Concept specifications (precise distinctions)
- **Scarcity ≠ poverty.** Billionaire's Tuesday is still scarce.
- **Scarcity ≠ shortage.** Shortages come and go; scarcity is the underlying condition that makes prices exist at all.
- **Opportunity cost ≠ accounting cost.** The factory's owned building isn't free; it's the foregone rent or alternative return.
- **Models are useful, not true.** Box: "all models are wrong; some are useful." Subway-map analogy.
- **Voluntary trade is positive-sum**, not zero-sum.

## Named tools (instruments invoked)
- Adam Smith's three mechanisms (comparative advantage, learning by doing, economies of scale).
- The circular flow diagram — two boxes, four arrows.
- George Box's "all models are wrong" line.
- Keynes's "apparatus of the mind" framing of economics-as-method (used implicitly in the synthesis).

## Trade-offs named
- **Opportunity cost** — chapter 2 will formalize.
- **Brittleness of specialization** — gains require working trade infrastructure; specialists fall harder when systems break.
- **Fidelity vs. clarity in models** — more detail = more accurate, less usable.
- **Coordination costs** of traditional/command/market systems — each handles different problems well; none is unambiguously best.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Economics is about money." → No, it's about choice under scarcity.
- "Specialization is unambiguously good." → No, it's the engine of abundance *and* the source of brittleness.
- "Trade is zero-sum." → No, when voluntary, it's positive-sum.
- "Models are wrong because they're simple." → Wrong reason; models are useful because they simplify.
