# Pantry — Chapter 27: Money and Banking

## Cold-open scenes
- Cowries, Yap stone disks, gold coins, paper, electronic, crypto — money's many disguises.
- 1971 U.S. abandonment of the gold standard.

## Worked examples
- Money multiplier: $1,000 base, 10% reserve → $10,000 deposits.
- Bank balance sheet construction.
- 1930–33 wave of bank failures (~9,000 banks).

## Concept specifications
- **Money's value is collective.** Acceptance is what gives it value.
- **Four functions:** medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, deferred payment standard.
- **Commodity money** has intrinsic value; **fiat money** doesn't.
- **M1 vs M2** — narrower vs broader liquidity definitions.
- **Money multiplier ≠ infinite.** Limited by reserve requirements, cash holdings, excess reserves.

## Named tools
- Money supply M1 and M2.
- Fractional-reserve banking.
- Money multiplier (1/reserve ratio).
- Bank balance sheet (assets, liabilities, equity).
- Deposit insurance (FDIC).
- Capital requirements.

## Trade-offs named
- Liquidity (depositors want it) vs. profitability (banks want to lend).
- Capital requirements (safety) vs. lending (growth).
- Deposit insurance (prevents runs) vs. moral hazard.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Money has to be backed by gold to be real." → No, fiat works; the value is collective.
- "Banks just hold our money." → No, they lend most of it out and create money in the process.
- "More money supply = more inflation." → Only when bank lending and velocity are operating; both can be depressed.
