## Concept specifications
- **Current account ≠ capital account**, but they sum to zero by identity.
- **Trade deficit = capital inflow.** No exception.
- **S − I = NX.** Trade balance equals savings-investment gap.
- **Trade policy ≠ trade balance.** Policy affects which goods, not balance.
- **Level of trade ≠ balance of trade.** Two separate questions.

## Named tools
- Balance of payments.
- Current account components (merchandise, services, income, transfers).
- Capital and financial account.
- Savings-investment identity.
- Reserve currency status (USD as global reserve).

## Trade-offs named
- Trade deficit funding investment vs. funding consumption.
- Capital inflow benefits (cheap funding) vs. risks (sudden reversal).
- Open trade (variety, scale) vs. exposure to international shocks.
- Surplus economies (export jobs) vs. trade frictions with deficit partners.

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Trade deficits mean we're losing." → No, they're capital inflows.
- "Tariffs reduce the trade deficit." → They redirect trade; the deficit reflects savings-investment gap.
- "Trade surpluses are always good." → They can reflect inadequate domestic investment opportunities.
