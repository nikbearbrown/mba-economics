## Concept specifications
- **Saving-investment identity** with public sector: private saving funds investment + deficit + net exports.
- **Twin deficits**: budget and trade deficits move together.
- **Ricardian equivalence**: theoretically, households save the tax cut anticipating future taxes. Partially holds.
- **Crowding out**: gov borrowing pushes up interest rates, reduces private investment.
- **Debt sustainability** depends on: r vs g, currency denomination, reserve status, political stability, demographics.

## Trade-offs named
- Deficit-financed stimulus benefits vs. crowding out and Ricardian offsets.
- Current consumption vs. future tax burden.
- Debt accumulation flexibility vs. long-run sustainability.
- Foreign capital benefits (cheap funding) vs. risks (sudden reversal).

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Deficits don't matter." → Modest deficits during slack often don't; chronic deficits during full employment do.
- "Crowding out always offsets stimulus." → Not when slack is large or monetary policy accommodates.
- "Debt is a fixed limit." → Limits depend on context (currency, growth, reserve status).
