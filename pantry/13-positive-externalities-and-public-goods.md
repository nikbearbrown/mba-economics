# Pantry — Chapter 13: Positive Externalities and Public Goods

## Cold-open scenes
- GPS — built by U.S. DoD, used free, ~30 satellites in MEO. Would never have been built privately.
- NASA Voyager program (1970s).

## Worked examples
- R&D rate-of-return gap: social 30–50%, private 7–15%.
- Lighthouse / radio broadcast / national defense as public-good canon.
- Fishery as common-pool resource (tragedy of the commons).
- Condo HOA dues as collective response to free-rider.

## Concept specifications
- **Positive externality:** benefit to third parties; market under-produces.
- **Patent ≠ free lunch.** Solves under-investment in R&D *by creating monopoly* — same dynamic-vs-static trade-off as in Ch 9.
- **Public good = non-rival + non-excludable.** Free-rider problem follows.
- **Common-pool resource = rival + non-excludable.** Tragedy of the commons.
- **Club good = non-rival + excludable.** Can be privately provided.

## Named tools
- Private vs social marginal benefit diagram.
- Patent / IP as monopoly grant.
- Subsidies and tax credits for R&D.
- Public provision.
- Four-quadrant typology (rivalry × excludability).

## Trade-offs named
- Patents: dynamic incentive vs. static monopoly cost.
- Public provision: bureaucratic capture vs. private under-provision.
- Coercive funding (taxes) vs. voluntary contribution (always under-funded).

## Counter-claims taken positions on
- "Government picks losers" → Sometimes. Track record is mixed; not uniformly bad.
- "Patents are pure giveaway to corporations" → No, the dynamic R&D incentive is real.
- "If something is needed, the market will produce it" → No, not for non-excludable goods.
