# Image briefs — Chapter 3: Demand and Supply

## Hero image
**Concept.** Two apples side by side on a wooden grocery shelf, with two price tags clearly visible: $2.75 and $1.72. The "organic" label on the left apple visible. Slightly diffuse natural light. Mood: a quiet question made visual.

## Figure 1 — Supply and demand curves with equilibrium
**Concept.** Standard two-curve diagram. Demand sloping down, supply sloping up, crossing at $P^*$, $Q^*$. Axes labeled. Equilibrium point marked with a dot.
**Where:** in §"The two curves," at the close.

## Figure 2 — Movement along vs. shift of demand
**Concept.** Two side-by-side panels. Left panel: a demand curve with two points marked, showing a movement along it caused by a price change. Right panel: two demand curves, the second shifted to the right of the first, with annotation "*shift caused by income rise.*" Same axes both panels.
**Where:** in §"Movements along, shifts of."

## Figure 3 — Four-step analysis worked: SUV market 2022
**Concept.** Four mini-panels. Panel 1: original SUV equilibrium. Panel 2: arrow indicating "gas prices rose, complement of SUVs." Panel 3: demand curve shifted left. Panel 4: new equilibrium at lower price and lower quantity, both old and new points visible.
**Where:** in §"The four-step analysis."

## Figure 4 — Price ceiling with shortage and deadweight loss
**Concept.** Demand-supply diagram with a horizontal line below equilibrium price labeled "*price ceiling*." Shaded region: deadweight-loss triangle. Bracket on horizontal axis showing the shortage gap (Q_demanded − Q_supplied at the ceiling).
**Where:** in §"Price controls and what they hide."

## Figure 5 — Surplus areas before and after a price ceiling
**Concept.** Two side-by-side panels of the same supply-demand diagram. Left: equilibrium with consumer surplus (light shading above price), producer surplus (light shading below price), and total surplus = both. Right: same diagram with binding price ceiling — shrunken consumer surplus, shrunken producer surplus, and a clearly marked deadweight-loss triangle that used to be filled.
**Where:** in §"Surplus" subsection.
