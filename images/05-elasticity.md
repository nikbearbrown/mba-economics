# Image briefs — Chapter 5: Elasticity

## Hero image
**Concept.** A close-up of a price tag being changed. Two sticker layers visible — the old price under a new one. Mood: a small, technical act with enormous downstream consequences. No faces.

## Figure 1 — Elastic, inelastic, unitary demand
**Concept.** Three side-by-side panels. Each shows a demand curve and a price increase. Panel 1 (elastic): steep loss of quantity for a small price rise. Panel 2 (inelastic): tiny loss of quantity for a big price rise. Panel 3 (unitary): proportional change. Annotations show elasticity values: 1.5, 0.4, 1.0.
**Where:** in §"What elasticity measures."

## Figure 2 — Perfect elasticity (horizontal demand) and perfect inelasticity (vertical demand)
**Concept.** Two side-by-side panels. Left: a flat horizontal demand curve at a given price. Right: a vertical demand curve at a given quantity. Each annotated with an example (commodity wheat for horizontal; insulin for vertical).
**Where:** at the end of §"What elasticity measures."

## Figure 3 — Short-run vs. long-run supply
**Concept.** Two demand-supply diagrams of the same gasoline market. Same demand. Left: short-run supply (steep, inelastic). Right: long-run supply (flatter, more elastic). Same demand shock applied — large price rise short run, smaller price rise long run.
**Where:** in §"Why elasticities differ."

## Figure 4 — Tax incidence: inelastic demand vs. inelastic supply
**Concept.** Two demand-supply diagrams. Both show a $1 per unit tax. Left panel: inelastic demand (cigarettes) — the tax wedge falls almost entirely on buyers. Right panel: inelastic supply (beachfront real estate) — the tax wedge falls almost entirely on sellers. Annotation showing the buyer-share and seller-share of the tax.
**Where:** in §"Tax incidence."

## Figure 5 — Total revenue and elasticity
**Concept.** Two side-by-side demand-curve panels. Left: inelastic demand, price increase, revenue rectangle gets bigger. Right: elastic demand, price increase, revenue rectangle gets smaller. Use shaded rectangles to show "revenue = price × quantity" before and after.
**Where:** in §"Elasticity and revenue."
