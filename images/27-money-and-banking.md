## Figure 1 — Money multiplier mechanism
**Concept.** Sequential flow diagram. Round 1: $1,000 deposit, 10% reserve, $900 lent. Round 2: $900 deposit, $90 reserve, $810 lent. Round 3: $810 deposit. Five rounds shown. Total deposits column shows the convergence to $10,000.
**Where:** in §"How banks create money."

## Figure 2 — Bank balance sheet T-account
**Concept.** Standard T-account format. Left column "Assets": Reserves $50M, Loans $850M, Securities $50M, Other $50M. Right column "Liabilities + Equity": Deposits $1,000M, Equity $50M (with smaller other items if needed). Labels showing the balance.
**Where:** in §"Reading a bank balance sheet."

## Figure 3 — M1 vs. M2 components
**Concept.** Two stacked bar charts. M1: cash ($2T), checking deposits, liquid savings. M2: M1 plus savings accounts, MMF, small CDs. Visual scale showing M2 ≈ M1 × 1.2 in current data.
**Where:** in §"Measuring the money supply."

## Figure 4 — U.S. monetary base vs. M2 since 2008
**Concept.** Two-line chart 2007–2024. Monetary base rising sharply 2008 onward (QE expansions). M2 rising more modestly. The gap indicates the falling money multiplier — banks holding excess reserves rather than lending.
**Where:** in §"How banks create money," after the multiplier-formula discussion.
