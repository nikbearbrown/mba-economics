# Image briefs — Chapter 16: Information, Risk, and Insurance

## Hero image
**Concept.** A row of used cars on a lot, viewed at dusk, prices visible on windshields. Mood: a market structured around what the buyer can't see. No people.

## Figure 1 — The lemons unraveling
**Concept.** A flow diagram. (1) Sellers know good vs lemon. (2) Buyers offer average price. (3) Good cars exit the market. (4) Average quality drops. (5) Buyers offer lower price. (6) Repeat until only lemons remain.
**Where:** in §"Asymmetric information — the lemons problem."

## Figure 2 — Adverse selection in insurance
**Concept.** Two density curves on the same axes. X-axis: risk level. Y-axis: number of people. Population distribution (broad bell curve) vs. those who voluntarily buy insurance (right-shifted distribution heavily weighted toward high-risk individuals).
**Where:** in §"Adverse selection."

## Figure 3 — Moral hazard
**Concept.** A two-panel diagram. Left: behavior without insurance (cautious driving, low accident probability). Right: behavior with insurance (less cautious, higher accident probability). Annotation: "Insurance changes the incentive."
**Where:** in §"Moral hazard."

## Figure 4 — Insurance pool mechanics
**Concept.** A schematic diagram. Many small individual losses (modeled as random draws) flow into a central premium pool. The pool pays out to those who suffer losses. Annotation: "Premiums × population = expected claims + administration + profit."
**Where:** in §"Insurance — pooling risk."
