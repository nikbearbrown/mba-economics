# Image briefs — Chapter 26: Neoclassical Perspective

## Hero image
**Concept.** A close-up of a Federal Reserve building façade with classical architecture visible. Mood: institutional gravity, the central bank as anchor. No people.

## Figure 1 — Volcker disinflation: fed funds rate vs. inflation
**Concept.** Two-line chart 1975–1990. Y-axis: percent. Lines: federal funds rate (rising sharply 1979–81 to ~20%) and CPI inflation (declining from ~14% to ~4% by 1985).
**Where:** in cold open or §"The neoclassical claim."

## Figure 2 — Lucas critique illustrated
**Concept.** A flow diagram. Step 1: Central bank announces "we'll accept higher inflation." Step 2: Rational firms/workers anticipate, build into contracts. Step 3: Inflation actually rises immediately. Step 4: No real GDP gain, only inflation.
**Where:** in §"Rational expectations."

## Figure 3 — Supply-side reforms shifting LRAS
**Concept.** AD-AS diagram. LRAS shifts right (representing growth in potential GDP). Same AD curve. New equilibrium: higher real GDP, lower price level. Annotation: "Supply-side reform raises long-run output."
**Where:** in §"Supply-side policy."

## Figure 4 — Modern synthesis: short-run and long-run combined
**Concept.** AD-AS diagram. SRAS three-zone. LRAS vertical. Initial equilibrium at full employment. Demand shock shifts AD left (recession). In the short run, equilibrium moves down the SRAS curve. In the long run, SRAS shifts as expectations and wages adjust, returning the economy to LRAS at lower price level. Time-arrows show the transition.
**Where:** in §"The modern synthesis."
