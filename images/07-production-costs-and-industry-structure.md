# Image briefs — Chapter 7: Production, Costs, and Industry Structure

## Hero image
**Concept.** A row of cardboard shipping boxes on a conveyor belt in a warehouse, slightly out of focus. One box centered, sharply lit. A barcode label visible. Mood: a quiet engine of industrial scale. No faces.

## Figure 1 — Production function with diminishing marginal product
**Concept.** Two-axis chart: total output (y) vs. labor input (x), with capital fixed. Curve rises, then flattens, then plateaus. Marginal product (separate panel) declines, then approaches zero, then turns negative.
**Where:** in §"The production function — inputs to outputs."

## Figure 2 — Short-run cost curves (the standard 7-curve picture)
**Concept.** Two-axis chart: cost on y-axis, output on x-axis. Show TFC (horizontal), TVC (rising), TC (TFC + TVC, rising). On separate per-unit panel: U-shaped AVC, U-shaped ATC (offset right), AFC (declining hyperbola), MC crossing both AVC and ATC at their minima.
**Where:** in §"Cost curves — the supply-side toolkit."

## Figure 3 — Long-run average cost curve
**Concept.** Wide U-shape. Three regions clearly labeled: economies of scale (downward slope), constant returns (flat region), diseconomies of scale (upward slope). Output on x-axis.
**Where:** in §"The long run — economies of scale."

## Figure 4 — Cost structure comparison: bookstore vs. Amazon
**Concept.** Two side-by-side LRAC curves. Left: brick-and-mortar bookstore — high fixed costs, modest variable costs, narrow scale advantage range. Right: Amazon — even higher fixed costs (warehouses, software) but extreme economies of scale, with average cost falling dramatically as output rises.
**Where:** in §"The long run — economies of scale," after the Amazon paragraph.
