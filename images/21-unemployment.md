## Figure 2 — Labor force participation rate 1948–2024
**Concept.** Time series. Y-axis: LFP %. X-axis: 1948–2024. Rising steeply 1960s–1990s, peaking near 67%, then declining to ~62% currently. Annotations marking COVID dip and partial recovery.
**Where:** in §"What unemployment means — and what the rate counts."

## Figure 3 — U-3 vs. U-6 comparison
**Concept.** Two lines on the same axes 2000–2024. U-3 (headline rate) lower; U-6 (broader) higher. Show how the gap widens during recessions.
**Where:** in §"What unemployment means — and what the rate counts."

## Figure 4 — Sticky wages diagram
**Concept.** Standard labor-supply-and-demand diagram. Initial equilibrium at wage W*, employment L*. Demand curve shifts left (recession). New competitive equilibrium would be at lower wage W' and lower employment L'. But the wage doesn't fall — it stays at W*. Result: surplus of labor = unemployment shown as the gap on the supply curve at W*.
**Where:** in §"Sticky wages — why unemployment exists at all."

## Figure 5 — Natural rate over time
**Concept.** A line tracking the U.S. natural rate of unemployment estimate over time, with shading for the uncertainty band. Line starts ~7% in late 1970s, falls to ~5% by 2000, ~4% currently. Source: Federal Reserve estimates.
**Where:** in §"The natural rate."
