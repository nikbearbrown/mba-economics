# Image briefs — Chapter 31: Government Borrowing

## Hero image
**Concept.** A close-up of a U.S. Treasury bond document or savings bond with the dollar sign visible. Mood: a piece of paper that obligates a future payment. No people.

## Figure 1 — Twin deficits trajectory
**Concept.** Two-line chart 1980–2024. Y-axis: % of GDP. X-axis: years. Lines: U.S. budget deficit (or surplus, with negative being deficit) and U.S. trade deficit. Both negative (deficits) and tracking together over much of the period.
**Where:** in §"The twin deficits."

## Figure 2 — Crowding out diagram
**Concept.** Capital market: vertical axis interest rate, horizontal axis quantity of capital. Original equilibrium. Government adds to demand for capital → demand shifts right. New equilibrium at higher rate, larger total quantity. Annotation: "Private borrowers slide up the demand curve; private investment falls."
**Where:** in §"Crowding out."

## Figure 3 — Saving-investment identity expanded
**Concept.** Schematic showing private saving as a flow into a financial-system pool, with three outflows: investment, government deficit, net exports. Each outflow labeled with its symbol from the identity.
**Where:** in §"The national saving and investment identity, expanded."

## Figure 4 — Debt-to-GDP comparison: U.S., Japan, Greece
**Concept.** Three-line time-series chart. Y-axis: government debt as % of GDP. X-axis: 1995–2024 or so. Japan rises through 200%+. Greece spikes around 2010. U.S. rises gradually then sharply.
**Where:** in §"Debt sustainability."
