# Image briefs — Chapter 4: Labor and Financial Markets

## Hero image
**Concept.** Two stethoscopes hanging on a hook in a hospital corridor, soft-lit, with a clipboard and a pen on a counter beneath. Mood: the everyday ground of a labor market that turns on whether enough people answer the phone at 3 a.m. No faces.

## Figure 1 — Labor market for RNs in Minneapolis-St. Paul, 2020
**Concept.** Standard supply-demand diagram. Vertical axis: hourly wage. Horizontal axis: number of RNs employed. Two equilibria shown — the pre-2020 baseline and the post-aging-+-ACA new equilibrium with demand shifted right. Annotations labeling the wage and quantity changes.
**Where:** in §"Labor as a market."

## Figure 2 — Minimum wage in a competitive labor market vs. monopsony
**Concept.** Side-by-side panels. Left panel: competitive labor market with minimum wage above equilibrium — surplus of labor (unemployment) shown. Right panel: monopsony labor market — minimum wage above the monopsony wage but below the competitive wage produces *higher* employment, not lower.
**Where:** in §"The minimum wage as price floor."

## Figure 3 — Financial-capital market
**Concept.** Vertical axis: interest rate. Horizontal axis: quantity of financial capital. Upward-sloping supply (savers), downward-sloping demand (borrowers), equilibrium interest rate marked. Annotations identifying the players: "households, foreign capital" on the supply side; "firms, governments, households for mortgages and consumer credit" on the demand side.
**Where:** in §"Money across time."

## Figure 4 — Crowding out diagram
**Concept.** Same financial-capital diagram. Demand curve shifts right (government borrowing increases). New equilibrium: higher rate, larger total quantity, but private borrowers slide *up* the demand curve and end up with less. Annotation showing the share of capital absorbed by government vs. share absorbed by private borrowers.
**Where:** in §"Money across time," after the worked example.

## Figure 5 — Usury cap as price ceiling
**Concept.** Financial-capital diagram with horizontal line below equilibrium representing the cap. Shortage-of-credit gap shown. Annotation: "Borrowers above the cap are out of the formal market."
**Where:** in §"Usury laws as price ceilings."
