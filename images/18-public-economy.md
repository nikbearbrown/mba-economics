# Image briefs — Chapter 18: Public Economy

## Hero image
**Concept.** A close-up of a tire tread on a wet street, water splashed up around it. Mood: a small everyday object whose pricing is shaped by political dynamics no driver thinks about. No people.

## Figure 1 — Concentrated benefits, dispersed costs
**Concept.** Two sides of a balance scale. Left: a small pile of large coins (concentrated beneficiaries). Right: thousands of tiny coins (dispersed cost-payers). Annotation: "Each side has equal mass — but the political pressure flows from the side that's organized."
**Where:** in §"Concentrated benefits, dispersed costs."

## Figure 2 — Median voter theorem
**Concept.** Horizontal axis: policy position from left to right. A normal-distribution curve representing voters by ideology. Two candidate dots converging on the median. Arrows showing each candidate's incentive to move toward the center.
**Where:** in §"The median voter theorem."

## Figure 3 — Voting paradox
**Concept.** Three columns labeled Voter 1, Voter 2, Voter 3, with their preference orderings (A>B>C, B>C>A, C>A>B). Below: three pairwise contests showing A beats B, B beats C, C beats A — a circular result.
**Where:** in §"Voting paradoxes — when no winner exists."

## Figure 4 — Pork-barrel spending illustration
**Concept.** Map of the U.S. with pins in many districts, each labeled with a different small project (a bridge, a research facility, a building). All connected by arrows pointing to a central "general tax revenue" pot.
**Where:** in §"Pork-barrel spending and logrolling."
