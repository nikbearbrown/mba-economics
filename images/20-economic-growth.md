# Image briefs — Chapter 20: Economic Growth

## Hero image
**Concept.** A loaf of bread on a wooden table beside an old daguerreotype-style image of a worker in 1850s factory clothing. Mood: how much one day's wages buys, then and now. No people in close detail.

## Figure 1 — World GDP per capita 1800–2024
**Concept.** Time series showing world GDP per capita (PPP-adjusted) from 1800 to today. Visible inflection around 1800 in Europe; emergence of Asia post-1960; convergence patterns visible for some countries. Source: Maddison Project Database.
**Where:** in §"Two centuries of growth."

## Figure 2 — Solow framework: capital deepening with diminishing returns
**Concept.** Two-axis chart. X-axis: capital per worker. Y-axis: output per worker. Curve rises and flattens — concave. Annotation: "Adding more capital eventually yields diminishing additions to output."
**Where:** in §"Capital deepening — and its limits."

## Figure 3 — Growth decomposition for the U.S.
**Concept.** Stacked bar chart showing average annual GDP growth ~3.0% decomposed into capital deepening (~1.0%), labor force growth (~0.7%), human capital (~0.4%), TFP (~0.9%). Source line.
**Where:** in §"Labor productivity — the engine."

## Figure 4 — Convergence vs. divergence
**Concept.** Scatter plot. X-axis: GDP per capita 1960. Y-axis: average annual growth 1960–2020. Each dot a country. Show the cluster: countries that started rich grew slowly; some that started poor grew very fast (East Asia); others that started poor stayed poor (parts of Sub-Saharan Africa). Trend line, but with substantial scatter.
**Where:** in §"Convergence — and where it doesn't happen."

## Figure 5 — U.S. labor productivity growth by decade
**Concept.** Bar chart. X-axis: decades from 1950s through 2020s. Y-axis: average annual labor productivity growth %. Decline visible from ~2.5% in 1950s–60s, dip in 70s–80s, recovery in 90s–early 2000s, slowdown post-2005.
**Where:** in §"The puzzle of slow productivity growth."
