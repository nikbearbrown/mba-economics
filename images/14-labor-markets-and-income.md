# Image briefs — Chapter 14: Labor Markets and Income

## Hero image
**Concept.** A line of hard hats hanging on a row of hooks at the entrance to a factory. Some new, some weathered. Soft fluorescent light. Mood: working-class labor markets where a generation's prospects depend on what happens at the bargaining table. No people.

## Figure 1 — College wage premium 1979–2024
**Concept.** Time series. Y-axis: earnings ratio (college:high school). X-axis: 1979–2024. Curve rises from 1.35 to 1.70. Shading for periods of widening (1980s, 1990s).
**Where:** in cold open or §"How a firm decides what to pay."

## Figure 2 — Marginal revenue product as labor demand
**Concept.** Two-axis chart: wage on y-axis, quantity of labor on x-axis. Downward-sloping MRP curve. Horizontal line at the market wage. Intersection determines the firm's optimal employment.
**Where:** in §"How a firm decides what to pay."

## Figure 3 — Monopsony in the labor market
**Concept.** Standard monopsony diagram. Upward-sloping labor supply curve. Above it, an even-steeper marginal-cost-of-labor curve. MRP curve crossing both. Monopsony hires at MC of labor = MRP, then pays the wage from the supply curve at that quantity. Annotation showing competitive employment and competitive wage for comparison.
**Where:** in §"Monopsony — when employers have wage-setting power."

## Figure 4 — Union effect on a labor market
**Concept.** Labor demand and supply curves. Union sets a wage above competitive level. New employment lower than competitive. Excluded workers (those willing to work at the higher wage but not hired) shown.
**Where:** in §"Unions — pushing back on the demand side."

## Figure 5 — U.S. union density 1955–2024
**Concept.** Time series chart. Y-axis: percent of workforce unionized. X-axis: 1955–2024. Line falls from ~35% to ~10%. Annotation noting the divergence between public-sector (rising or stable) and private-sector (steeply falling) trends.
**Where:** in §"Unions" near the end.
