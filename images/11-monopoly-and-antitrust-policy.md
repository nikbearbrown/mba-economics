## Hero image

## Figure 1 — HHI illustration with three example markets
**Concept.** Three side-by-side bar charts showing market-share distributions for three hypothetical markets. Above each, the HHI value. Annotation explaining what each value would mean by DOJ thresholds.

## Figure 2 — Standard Oil 1911 breakup tree
**Concept.** Schematic family tree. Standard Oil at the top. 34 successor firms branching down (a few major ones labeled: Exxon predecessor, Mobil predecessor, Chevron predecessor, etc.). Year 1911 highlighted.
**Where:** in §"Three categories of enforcement," at the start.

## Figure 3 — Cost-plus vs. price-cap regulation
**Concept.** Two side-by-side panels. Left: cost-plus — diagram showing the firm's costs rising over time as it gold-plates equipment, with the regulated price following the costs up. Right: price-cap — diagram showing a fixed price ceiling and the firm's costs falling over time as it cuts costs to keep margin. Each panel annotated with the incentive direction.
**Where:** in §"The natural-monopoly dilemma — and how to regulate."

## Figure 4 — Regulatory capture mechanism
**Concept.** A circular diagram showing the four feedback loops of capture: (1) regulators depend on industry for information; (2) regulators socialize with industry executives; (3) regulators hope to work in industry post-government; (4) industry funds the political process that staffs regulators. Center: "Capture."
**Where:** in §"Regulatory capture."
