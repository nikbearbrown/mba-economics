# Image briefs — Chapter 24: AD-AS Model

## Hero image
**Concept.** A half-finished housing-development frame — wood studs against sky. Mood: an investment that started big and stopped. No people.

## Figure 1 — AD curve and its components
**Concept.** Two-axis chart. Y-axis: price level. X-axis: real GDP. Downward-sloping AD curve. Annotation showing the three mechanisms (wealth, interest rate, net export) that produce the slope.
**Where:** in §"Aggregate Demand."

## Figure 2 — Three-zone SRAS curve
**Concept.** Two-axis chart. SRAS curve with clearly visible three zones — flat on the left (Keynesian), upward-sloping in the middle (intermediate), nearly vertical on the right (neoclassical). Annotations labeling each zone and the underlying conditions (slack, tightening, full capacity).
**Where:** in §"Short-run Aggregate Supply."

## Figure 3 — AD-AS equilibrium
**Concept.** Both curves on same axes. Equilibrium at the crossing. Price level P* and real GDP Y* labeled.
**Where:** in §"Equilibrium and shifts."

## Figure 4 — Stagflation: SRAS shift left
**Concept.** AD-AS diagram. Initial equilibrium. SRAS shifts left (oil shock). New equilibrium has higher price level and lower real GDP. Both effects clearly labeled.
**Where:** in §"Equilibrium and shifts," in the supply-shock example.

## Figure 5 — LRAS and the long-run framework
**Concept.** Vertical LRAS at potential GDP. Multiple AD curves at different positions, each producing different short-run equilibria but all converging to the same Y* in the long run.
**Where:** in §"Long-run Aggregate Supply."
