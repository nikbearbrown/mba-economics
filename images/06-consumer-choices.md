# Image briefs — Chapter 6: Consumer Choices

## Hero image
**Concept.** A small open lipstick on a desk next to a stack of bills. A single price tag visible. Mood: a small luxury at a moment when budgets are tight. No faces.

## Figure 1 — Diminishing marginal utility (table or curve)
**Concept.** A simple table with quantity (1–6) and total utility, marginal utility columns. Marginal column declining. Or a downward-curving marginal utility line.
**Where:** in §"The textbook model — utility maximization."

## Figure 2 — Substitution and income effects
**Concept.** Budget-constraint diagram with two budget lines and two consumption points. The shift from the original to the new bundle decomposed into the substitution effect (along original utility level) and income effect (parallel shift to new utility level).
**Where:** in §"Substitution and income effects."

## Figure 3 — Intertemporal budget constraint
**Concept.** Two-axis diagram: consumption now (x-axis), consumption later (y-axis). Downward-sloping line with slope −(1+r). Different lines for different interest rates — steeper lines = higher r.
**Where:** in §"Choices across time."

## Figure 4 — Prospect theory value function
**Concept.** S-shaped curve: gains and losses on x-axis, perceived value on y-axis. Loss side steeper than gain side. Annotation: "Losses feel about twice as bad as equivalent gains."
**Where:** in §"Loss aversion."

## Figure 5 — Auto-enrollment vs. opt-in 401(k) participation
**Concept.** Bar chart comparing 401(k) participation rates: traditional opt-in (~50%) vs. auto-enrollment (~90%). Source line: published 401(k) studies.
**Where:** in §"Present bias."
