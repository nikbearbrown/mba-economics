# Image briefs — Chapter 15: Poverty and Economic Inequality

## Hero image
**Concept.** A row of mailboxes outside an apartment building, varying in size and condition. Mood: addresses, fixed places where economic outcomes land. No people.

## Figure 1 — U.S. poverty rate 1959–2024
**Concept.** Time series. Y-axis: poverty rate (%). X-axis: 1959–2024. Line falls sharply 1959–1973 (22%→11%), then fluctuates 11–15% with no sustained trend. COVID years marked.
**Where:** in §"Poverty — measuring deprivation."

## Figure 2 — U.S. income share of top 1% over time
**Concept.** Time series 1913–2023. Top 1% pre-tax income share. Visible drop in the postwar decades, sharp rise from ~10% in 1980 to ~20% by 2010s. Source: Piketty/Saez tax data.
**Where:** in cold open or §"Inequality — measuring the spread."

## Figure 3 — Lorenz curve
**Concept.** Square diagram. Diagonal 45° line of perfect equality. Bowed-below Lorenz curve representing actual U.S. income distribution. Gini = 0.40 annotated as the area between the diagonal and the curve, scaled.
**Where:** in §"Inequality."

## Figure 4 — Poverty trap and EITC compared
**Concept.** Two side-by-side income diagrams.
- Left: classic safety net with 100% benefit-reduction. The household's net income stays flat as earnings rise from $0 to threshold.
- Right: EITC structure. Net income rises smoothly with earnings, with the credit increasing then phasing out gradually.
Annotation: "EITC removes the trap by changing the marginal incentive."
**Where:** in §"The poverty trap."

## Figure 5 — Cross-country Gini comparison
**Concept.** Bar chart. Y-axis: Gini coefficient. X-axis: country. Show ~15 OECD countries plus a few extreme cases. U.S. ~0.40, Sweden ~0.27, Brazil ~0.53. Visual sort by Gini.
**Where:** in §"Inequality" near the end.
