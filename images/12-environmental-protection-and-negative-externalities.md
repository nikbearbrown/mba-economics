# Image briefs — Chapter 12: Environmental Protection and Negative Externalities

## Hero image
**Concept.** A row of London chimneys disappearing into low fog, viewed from a rooftop. Late afternoon, almost monochrome. Mood: industrial production whose costs leave the page. No people.

## Figure 1 — Private vs. social cost diagram
**Concept.** Supply-demand diagram with two supply curves: private MC (lower) and social MC (higher, shifted up by the external cost per unit). Market quantity Q_market (where private MC meets demand) is greater than socially efficient Q* (where social MC meets demand). The deadweight-loss triangle shaded.
**Where:** in §"What an externality is."

## Figure 2 — Three policy instruments compared
**Concept.** Three small panels, each showing the externality diagram with a different intervention.
- Command-and-control: hard cap at Q*, no price signal shown.
- Pigouvian tax: tax wedge raises private MC to social MC.
- Cap-and-trade: permit price emerges, equivalent to the tax.
**Where:** in §"Pollution taxes" and §"Cap-and-trade," used to compare the three.

## Figure 3 — Marginal abatement cost curve
**Concept.** A rising curve showing the marginal cost of additional pollution reduction. Horizontal line at the Pigouvian tax level, intersecting the curve at the optimal abatement quantity. Annotation: "Firms reduce up to where marginal abatement cost equals the tax."
**Where:** in §"Pollution taxes."

## Figure 4 — U.S. SO₂ emissions, 1980–2020
**Concept.** Time series chart showing annual U.S. sulfur dioxide emissions from 1980 through 2020. Mark the 1990 Clean Air Act amendments. Show the steep decline that followed. Source: EPA.
**Where:** in §"Cap-and-trade."

## Figure 5 — International externality / free-rider diagram
**Concept.** Stylized cartoon-diagram showing carbon emissions from one country crossing a national border into the global atmosphere, with countries individually deciding whether to reduce. Annotation: "Each country prefers others to bear the cost. Without coordination, all under-reduce."
**Where:** in §"International externalities — the hardest case."
