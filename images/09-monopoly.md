# Image briefs — Chapter 9: Monopoly

## Hero image
**Concept.** A wooden chest of tea with the East India Company stamp visible, sitting on a dock at twilight. The Boston harbor in soft focus behind. Mood: a corporate monopoly that became a flashpoint. No people.

## Figure 1 — Monopoly demand and marginal revenue
**Concept.** Two-axis diagram: price (y), quantity (x). Downward-sloping market demand. Below it, a steeper marginal revenue curve. Annotation: "MR < P because lowering price to sell one more unit cuts revenue on every previously sold unit."
**Where:** in §"How a monopoly decides."

## Figure 2 — Monopoly profit maximization
**Concept.** Standard monopoly diagram. MR = MC determines Q_m. The price P_m is read off the demand curve at Q_m. The profit rectangle: width Q_m, height (P_m − ATC at Q_m). All clearly labeled.
**Where:** in §"How a monopoly decides," after the worked example.

## Figure 3 — Deadweight loss from monopoly vs. perfect competition
**Concept.** One diagram with both outcomes overlaid. Monopoly point at (Q_m, P_m). Competitive point at (Q_c, P_c) where demand crosses MC. Deadweight loss triangle shaded between Q_m and Q_c.
**Where:** in §"What it costs the rest of us."

## Figure 4 — Natural monopoly regulation dilemma
**Concept.** Cost curves with LRAC declining throughout the relevant range; MC below LRAC throughout. Demand curve crossing the LRAC and MC. Three pricing options labeled: unregulated monopoly (P > AC), AC pricing (P = AC, breakeven), MC pricing (P < AC, requires subsidy). Each shows the consequence.
**Where:** in §"A useful contrast — and a complication."
