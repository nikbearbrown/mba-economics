# Image briefs — Chapter 17: Financial Markets

## Hero image
**Concept.** A close-up of a stock-market ticker, mid-stream, with several symbols and prices visible. Slightly out of focus to emphasize motion. Mood: information moving faster than any individual can read it.

## Figure 1 — Four sources of corporate financing
**Concept.** A diagram with a central firm box. Four arrows pointing in: reinvested profits (from prior earnings), bank loans (from banks), bonds (from bond market investors), stocks (from equity investors). Each arrow labeled with the kind of claim or repayment.
**Where:** in §"How firms raise money."

## Figure 2 — Bond price and interest-rate inverse
**Concept.** Two-axis chart. X-axis: market interest rate. Y-axis: bond price (% of face value). Curve sloping down. Specific points marked: at 4% rate, bond at $1,022; at 5%, bond at $1,000; at 6%, bond at $979 (illustrative).
**Where:** in §"Bonds — promises to pay."

## Figure 3 — Risk-return scatter for major asset classes
**Concept.** Scatter plot. X-axis: standard deviation (risk). Y-axis: average annual real return. Points: U.S. Treasuries (low/low), corporate bonds (medium/medium), U.S. stock index (high/high), emerging-market stocks (very high/very high). Trend line showing the risk-return relationship.
**Where:** in §"Stocks — claims on whatever's left."

## Figure 4 — Diversification reduces variance
**Concept.** Line chart. X-axis: number of stocks in portfolio. Y-axis: portfolio variance. Curve falls steeply at first (single stock to ~10 stocks), then flattens around 30+ stocks toward an asymptote of "market risk." Annotation: "Idiosyncratic risk is diversifiable; market risk is not."
**Where:** in §"Diversification — the only free lunch."

## Figure 5 — U.S. home equity 2000–2012
**Concept.** Time series. Y-axis: total U.S. home equity (trillions of dollars). X-axis: 2000–2012. Peak at $13T in 2006, trough at ~$6T in 2009, recovery beginning in 2012.
**Where:** in §"The financial system's other side."
