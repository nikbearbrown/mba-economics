# Image briefs — Chapter 2: Choice in a World of Scarcity

## Hero image
**Concept.** A pair of hands at a table, a small pile of dollar bills and a stack of receipts, with two paths visible: one toward a textbook and a graduation cap, one toward work boots and a lunch pail. Soft light from a window. Mood: a real decision in a real life, neither sentimentalized nor academic.
**Constraints.** No faces. No symbols-of-the-future. Just the present moment of choice.

## Figure 1 — Alphonso's budget constraint
**Concept.** Two-axis line graph. Vertical: burgers (0–5). Horizontal: bus tickets (0–20). A straight line from (0, 5) to (20, 0). Labeled points A through F at intervals along the line. Slope annotation: "−0.25 burgers per bus ticket (or −4 bus tickets per burger)."
**Where:** in §"The shape of an individual choice," after the setup paragraph.

## Figure 2 — Diminishing marginal utility (pizza)
**Concept.** Bar chart, x-axis = "slice number 1 through 8," y-axis = "satisfaction." Bars descend from a tall first slice to a near-zero or negative eighth. Annotation: "Marginal utility decreases as you eat more, even of a thing you love."
**Where:** in §"How people decide on the surface."

## Figure 3 — Production possibilities frontier (healthcare vs. education)
**Concept.** Outward-bowing curve. Vertical: healthcare. Horizontal: education. Points A (all-healthcare), B, C, D, F (all-education) marked along the curve. Point R inside the curve, labeled "productively inefficient." Annotation: slope at A flat, slope at F steep — illustrating the law of increasing opportunity cost.
**Where:** in §"Scale up: the surface of society's choice."

## Figure 4 — Comparative advantage (US vs. Brazil PPFs)
**Concept.** Two PPFs side by side. Vertical: wheat. Horizontal: sugarcane. U.S. PPF is wide on the wheat axis, narrow on sugarcane. Brazil PPF is wide on sugarcane, narrow on wheat. Slope annotations show that wheat is "cheap" (in sugarcane terms) in the U.S., "expensive" in Brazil — and vice versa.
**Where:** in §"Scale up," at the end after the comparative-advantage paragraph.

Same as Ch 1. Two colors plus neutral. Tabular monospace for numbers. Captions open with what the reader is looking at, then the takeaway.
