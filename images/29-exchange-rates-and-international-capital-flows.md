# Image briefs — Chapter 29: Exchange Rates and International Capital Flows

## Hero image
**Concept.** A row of currency notes from different countries (dollar, euro, yen, yuan, pound) fanned out on a dark surface. Mood: a market of moneys. No people.

## Figure 1 — Major exchange rates 2000–2024
**Concept.** Time series. Three lines on same axes: USD/EUR, USD/JPY, USD/GBP. Y-axis: amount of foreign currency per dollar. X-axis: 2000–2024. Visible volatility and long-run trends.
**Where:** in cold open or §"What an exchange rate is."

## Figure 2 — FX market: supply and demand for the dollar
**Concept.** Standard supply-demand diagram. X-axis: quantity of dollars. Y-axis: dollar exchange rate (yen per dollar). Equilibrium where supply meets demand. Annotation showing what the curves represent (foreign demand for dollars to buy U.S. exports/assets vs. domestic supply of dollars to buy imports/foreign assets).
**Where:** in §"What determines the exchange rate."

## Figure 3 — The trilemma
**Concept.** Triangle diagram. Three corners: capital mobility, fixed exchange rate, monetary policy independence. Each side of the triangle marked with which two-of-three combination is feasible: U.S. (capital mobility + monetary independence, floating rate), Hong Kong (capital mobility + fixed rate, no monetary independence), China historically (fixed rate + monetary independence, capital controls).
**Where:** in §"Exchange-rate regimes," after the four-regime discussion.

## Figure 4 — Big Mac Index PPP comparison
**Concept.** Bar chart. Each bar a country. Bar height shows the Big Mac price in USD-equivalent at the actual exchange rate. Reference line at the U.S. price. Bars above the line = currency overvalued vs. PPP; bars below = undervalued.
**Where:** in §"Purchasing power parity."
