# Image briefs — Chapter 25: Keynesian Perspective

## Hero image
**Concept.** A 1930s-style breadline (in suggestion only — silhouettes, no faces). Mood: the macroeconomic problem Keynes addressed. Soft sepia tones.

## Figure 1 — Multiplier mechanism
**Concept.** A flow diagram. $100B stimulus → $70B respending → $49B → $34.3B → ... totaling $333B. Each round labeled with the share that gets re-spent.
**Where:** in §"The expenditure multiplier."

## Figure 2 — Multiplier with leakages
**Concept.** Same flow diagram but with three leakage arrows at each round (taxes, savings, imports). Total ends up smaller — annotated as "real-world multiplier."
**Where:** in §"The expenditure multiplier."

## Figure 3 — Short-run vs. long-run Phillips curve
**Concept.** Two-axis chart. Y-axis: inflation rate. X-axis: unemployment rate. A short-run Phillips curve sloping down. A vertical line at the natural rate (long-run). When expectations rise, the short-run curve shifts up. Annotation showing how the 1970s stagflation can be explained by this shift.
**Where:** in §"The Phillips Curve."

## Figure 4 — Discretionary vs. automatic stabilizers
**Concept.** Bar chart showing GDP shock response: "without any policy" (large drop), "with automatic stabilizers only" (somewhat smaller drop), "with automatic + discretionary" (smaller still). Source: standard CBO multiplier estimates.
**Where:** in §"Fiscal stabilization tools."
